Book digests/Building Microservices

Building Microservices 0: Preface

Source: Sam Newman, Building Microservices, 2nd ed. (O'Reilly, 2021), Preface. Next: Building Microservices 1: What Are Microservices?.

Takeaways
  • Microservices are an approach to distributed systems built from finely grained services that can be changed, deployed and released independently.
  • They suit organisations moving to loosely coupled systems with autonomous teams, but as a distributed system they bring significant complexity.
  • The book is neither pro nor anti microservices: it explores the context where they work well and the problems they cause. The author thinks microservices becoming "the default architectural choice" is hard to justify.
  • The second edition adds information hiding as a foundation, more worked examples, three chapters on inter-process communication, and separate build and deployment chapters (containers, Kubernetes, serverless).
  • Three parts: Foundation, Implementation, People.

0.1 Who should read it

  • The scope is broad: design, development, deployment, testing and maintenance.
  • It suits people already decomposing a monolith or building greenfield systems, and people deciding whether microservices are right for them.

0.2 Why a second edition

  1. Newman felt he could do a better job: more learned, a better writer.
  2. He helped bring these ideas into the mainstream, so he has a duty of care to present them in a sensible, balanced way.

The first edition was written in about a year (from early 2014, released February 2015), early in the microservices story. He wrote the first edition from a fairly unbiased viewpoint because he did not work for a big technology vendor and was not selling a solution.

0.3 What changed since the first edition

  • Wider industry experience and more technology to explore.
  • Ideas at the edge of his thinking, like information hiding, became foundational concepts worth highlighting.
  • Seeing many teams flock to Kubernetes hoping it would solve all their microservice problems "gave me pause for thought".
  • Security and resiliency expanded.
  • More explicit examples; every chapter re-examined, every sentence reviewed.
  • Inter-process communication now spans three chapters; separate build and deployment chapters reflect containers, Kubernetes and serverless.
  • The goal was a book of the same size with more ideas; "this edition is bigger".

0.4 Navigating the book

  • Organised by topic, written to be read start to finish, but you can jump to a chapter. The Glossary explains unfamiliar terms; key advice is summarised in the Bibliography.
  • The book uses microservice and service interchangeably unless it says otherwise.

0.5 About the writing

Written through 2020 and the first half of 2021 (largely during the pandemic), in Visual Studio Code on macOS, with OmniGraffle for all diagrams and AsciiDoc with O'Reilly's Atlas toolchain for formatting. For the second edition Martin Fowler returned as technical reviewer, joined by Daniel Bryant and Sarah Wells.

Summary points

  • Microservices are powerful but complex; the book weighs context, benefits and costs rather than advocating.
  • Read the three parts in order: concepts (Foundation), techniques (Implementation), organisation (People).
Terms to carry forward
  • Microservice / service: used interchangeably in the book.
  • Information hiding: a foundational concept highlighted in this edition.
  • BFF, GraphQL, stream-aligned and enabling teams: previewed for Part III.
