Monolith to Microservices 6: Closing Words

Source: Sam Newman, Monolith to Microservices (O'Reilly, 2019), chapter 6. Previous: Monolith to Microservices 5: Growing Pains.

Takeaways
  • Two key messages: make rational decisions (give yourself space and information, think about your own context rather than copying others, assess options, stay open to change) and adopt incrementally.
  • No two microservice architectures are alike; learn from others but find what works in your context.
  • Breaking the journey into manageable steps gives the best chance of success because you can adapt as you go.
  • Microservices are definitely not for everyone.

6.1 The two messages

  1. Decide rationally. Give yourself enough space and gather the right information. Do not just copy others: think about your problem and context, assess the options, move forward, and be open to changing course later (the three questions in Monolith to Microservices 2: Planning a Migration).
  2. Adopt incrementally. Incremental adoption of microservices, and of the associated technologies and practices, is the key (the patterns in Monolith to Microservices 3: Splitting the Monolith and Monolith to Microservices 4: Decomposing the Database).

6.2 Final points

  • No two architectures are alike, so lessons from other organisations need adapting.
  • Small steps let you adapt your approach as you go.
  • Microservices are not for everyone; the aim of the book is to help you judge whether they suit you and, if so, how to start.
