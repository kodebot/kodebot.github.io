Learning DDD 17: Closing Words

Source: Vlad Khononov, Learning Domain-Driven Design (O'Reilly, 2021), closing words. Previous: Learning DDD 16: Data Mesh. Next: Learning DDD 18: Appendix A, Applying DDD: A Case Study.

Takeaways
  • The book follows one quote: "There is no sense in talking about the solution before we agree on the problem, and no sense talking about the implementation steps before we agree on the solution" (Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag): problem, solution, implementation.
  • Take away the logic and principles behind the tools, not DDD as dogma.
  • "Always watch your ubiquitous language, and when in doubt, do EventStorming."

Problem

  • To build a software solution, first understand the problem: the business domain, goals and strategy.
  • The ubiquitous language gives deep understanding of the domain and its logic
    • Bounded contexts break the complexity apart, each implementing a single model aimed at a specific problem
    • The building blocks of domains are core, supporting and generic subdomains:
Subdomain Competitive advantage Complexity Volatility Implementation Problem
Core Yes High High In-house Interesting
Generic No High Low Buy or adopt Solved
Supporting No Low Low In-house or outsource Obvious

Solution

  • Optimise the design per subdomain type:
    • four business logic patterns (transaction script, active record, domain model, event-sourced domain model) and three architectural patterns (layered architecture, ports and adapters, CQRS)
    • Figure E-1 is the decision tree summarising the tactical heuristics (see Learning DDD 10: Design Heuristics).

Implementation

  • Part III: build a ubiquitous language through EventStorming, keep the design in shape as the domain evolves, and introduce DDD on brownfield projects.
  • Part IV: the interplay with microservices, event-driven architecture and data mesh: DDD works with them, and they complement each other.

Area Book Why

Martraire (2019), Living Documentation DDD-based approach to knowledge sharing, documentation and testing
Vernon (2013), Implementing Domain-Driven Design Classic with in-depth discussion and detailed examples of strategic and tactical tools, built on a real example of failing DDD initiatives and their course corrections
Young (2017), Versioning in an Event Sourced System (Leanpub) Evolving event-sourced systems (chapter 7)

Hohpe and Woolf (2003), Enterprise Integration Patterns Origin of many chapter 9 patterns
Richardson (2019), Microservice Patterns (Manning) Detailed pattern examples incl. saga, process manager, outbox

Dates as printed

"Expected" and "not yet published" refer to the time of the book's writing (2021); check the current status yourself (unverified).

Conclusion

  • Take away the logic and principles behind the tools. Do not follow DDD blindly as dogma
    • understanding the reasoning increases your chances of applying it, and leveraging individual concepts is key especially on brownfield projects.
  • "Always watch your ubiquitous language, and when in doubt, do EventStorming. Good luck!"
Terms to carry forward
  • Problem, solution, implementation: the book's three-part arc.
